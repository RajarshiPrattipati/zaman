Kitchen Renovation in a Dubai Apartment: What to Plan For

Kitchen renovations in apartments are different from villas. You share walls, floors, drainage and electrical risers with your neighbours, and the building has its own rules about what you can change and when. Planning around those limits from the start saves time and avoids work being stopped halfway.

Approvals and building rules for kitchen renovations

Start with permission, not design.

  • Tenants need written approval from the landlord. Many landlords allow new cabinet doors or appliances but not a full replacement or layout change.
  • Owners usually need approval from building management or the owners association. Expect to submit a description of the work, sometimes a layout drawing, and your contractor's trade license.
  • Changes to gas, plumbing, drainage or electrical supply can need further approvals or permits. Check with building management before you finalise the design.

Buildings also set rules for renovation work. Common ones include fixed working hours, no noisy work on certain days, booking the service lift for deliveries and debris, and protecting common corridors. Your contractor needs to know these rules before quoting, because they affect how long the job takes.

What you can and cannot easily move

In an apartment, the position of the sink is usually tied to the drainage stack. Moving the sink a short distance along the same wall is often possible. Moving it to an island or the opposite wall is much harder, because the waste pipe needs a continuous fall, and you usually cannot cut into the slab below.

Similar limits apply to:

  • Kitchen exhaust. The cooker hood may need to connect to an existing duct or vent. If there is none, a recirculating hood with filters is the practical option.
  • Gas supply. Where a building has piped gas, any changes must be made by an approved provider. Many apartments use electric cooking instead.
  • Washing machine and dishwasher points. These need both a water supply and a drain, so they are best kept near the sink.

Keeping wet points in roughly the same place keeps the job simpler and lowers the chance of leaks.

Electrical load and kitchen sockets

Modern kitchens draw a lot of power. An oven, induction hob, dishwasher, microwave, kettle and coffee machine can all run at once. Older apartment kitchens were often wired for less.

Points to check with an electrician:

  • Whether the existing circuits can take the new appliances, or whether dedicated circuits are needed for the oven and hob
  • Enough sockets above the counter, so you are not using extension leads
  • Sockets and switches kept a safe distance from the sink
  • Proper earthing and protection on the kitchen circuits
  • Under-cabinet lighting and whether it needs its own switch

If you notice warm sockets, tripping breakers or flickering lights in the existing kitchen, read our guide on home electrical warning signs and fix the cause during the renovation.

Choosing cabinets and countertops for Dubai kitchens

Kitchens in Dubai deal with heat, humidity from cooking, and in some buildings, occasional leaks from above or behind. Choose materials that cope.

Cabinets

  • Carcasses under the sink should be moisture-resistant board, because this is where small leaks start.
  • Doors come in many finishes. Lacquer looks smooth but can chip. Laminates and foils are practical and easy to clean.
  • Hinges and drawer runners take the most wear. Soft-close hardware of decent quality is worth the cost.
  • Plinths with a moisture-resistant finish help protect the cabinets from mopping water.

Countertops

  • Quartz is hard-wearing and does not need sealing.
  • Granite is heat-resistant and durable but needs occasional sealing.
  • Laminate is the budget option and can be damaged by hot pans and standing water at joints.
  • Solid wood needs regular oiling and is not ideal next to the sink.

Order of work for an apartment kitchen

  1. Approvals, protection of floors and common areas, and lift booking
  2. Remove old cabinets, counters and tiles
  3. Plumbing and electrical first fix, including any new circuits
  4. Wall repairs, then tiling or splashback preparation
  5. Gypsum ceiling work and lighting points
  6. Install base and wall cabinets
  7. Template and fit countertops
  8. Splashback, sink, taps and appliances
  9. Final electrical fittings, painting and snagging

Measuring for countertops after the cabinets are fixed avoids gaps and bad joints.
