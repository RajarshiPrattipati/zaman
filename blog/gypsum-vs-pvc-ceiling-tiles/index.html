Gypsum vs PVC vs Mineral Fibre Ceiling Tiles: Which to Choose

Tiled ceilings sit in a metal grid hung below the slab. Each tile lifts out, so you can reach cables, ducts and pipes without cutting anything. That is why they are standard in offices, shops, clinics, warehouses and back-of-house areas.

The three tile types you will be offered most often in Dubai are gypsum, PVC and mineral fibre. They look similar from the floor but behave differently once they are installed. This guide explains the trade-offs so you can pick the right one for each space.

How a grid ceiling works

A suspended grid ceiling has three parts: hangers fixed to the slab, a grid of main and cross tees, and tiles that drop into the grid. The grid is usually white powder-coated steel, though other colours and profiles are available.

Because the tiles are loose-laid, a grid ceiling is fast to install and easy to maintain. The trade-off is appearance. You will always see the grid lines, which suits a working space better than a living room. For homes, a jointed gypsum false ceiling usually looks more finished.

Gypsum ceiling tiles

Gypsum tiles are gypsum board cut to size, usually with a vinyl or painted face. They are rigid, flat and fire-resistant.

Good for: offices, corridors, retail units and general areas where you want a clean, solid look.

Strengths:

  • Firm and flat, so they don't bow easily.
  • Good fire performance compared with plastic.
  • Vinyl-faced versions wipe clean.

Watch for:

  • Standard gypsum absorbs water. A small leak above can stain or sag a tile. Moisture-resistant versions exist and are worth it near pantries or washrooms.
  • They are heavier than PVC, so the grid and hangers need to be set up properly.
  • Edges can chip if tiles are lifted roughly during maintenance.

PVC ceiling tiles

PVC tiles are lightweight plastic panels. They don't absorb water and they clean easily with a damp cloth.

Good for: kitchens, food preparation areas, washrooms, cold stores, car wash bays and other wet or hygiene-sensitive spaces.

Strengths:

  • Fully moisture-proof and resistant to mould.
  • Very light, which makes handling and installation simple.
  • Easy to clean, which matters in food and clinical areas.

Watch for:

  • Lower fire performance than gypsum or mineral fibre. Check what is acceptable for your premises with your building management or fit-out consultant before choosing.
  • Thin tiles can warp near heat sources such as strong lights or ovens.
  • They don't absorb sound, so open offices can feel echoey.

Mineral fibre ceiling tiles

Mineral fibre tiles are made from compressed mineral wool and binders. They have a soft, textured face and are the standard choice for many commercial fit-outs.

Good for: open-plan offices, meeting rooms, classrooms, clinics and anywhere noise control matters.

Strengths:

  • Good sound absorption, which reduces echo and makes speech clearer.
  • Generally good fire performance.
  • Many textures and edge details available.

Watch for:

  • They absorb moisture and can sag or stain in humid areas. Keep them away from wet rooms unless the product is rated for it.
  • The surface marks easily from dirty hands, so handle them with gloves.
  • Cutting creates dust, so plan the work around occupied areas.

Choosing by space

A single project often uses more than one type. A practical approach:

  • Open office and meeting rooms: mineral fibre for acoustics.
  • Reception and corridors: gypsum tiles for a crisp look, or a jointed gypsum ceiling if the budget allows.
  • Pantry, washrooms, kitchens: PVC or moisture-resistant gypsum.
  • Retail floor: gypsum or mineral fibre, depending on whether the space is noisy.
  • Warehouse offices and storage: whatever suits your budget and maintenance needs, as long as it meets fire requirements.

If you are also planning new internal walls, read our guide to gypsum partition walls for offices so the ceiling and partitions are planned together.

Practical tips for any tile ceiling

  • Fix lights, diffusers and sensors to the grid or their own supports, not just resting on tiles.
  • Keep a box of spare tiles from the same batch. Replacement tiles from a later batch can differ slightly in colour.
  • Mark tiles under valves, AC units and junction boxes so maintenance staff know where to lift.
  • Check with your landlord or building management about approval before changing a ceiling in a commercial unit, especially where sprinklers or fire alarms are involved.

When to call a contractor

Replacing a few stained tiles is a simple job once the leak above is fixed. Re-gridding a room, changing tile types across a floor, or moving lights and AC grilles needs a team that can coordinate ceiling, electrical and AC work together.
