Most electrical problems give some warning before they become dangerous. The trouble is that the early signs are easy to live with: a breaker that trips now and then, a socket that feels a little warm, a light that flickers on hot afternoons. This guide explains what those signs can mean and what you can safely do about them.

One rule applies throughout: electrical repairs need a qualified electrician. Do not open the distribution board (DB), remove socket faceplates, or try to trace wiring yourself.

## A breaker that keeps tripping

Breakers and earth leakage devices in your DB board are there to cut power when something is wrong. A single trip after a storm or a power cut is not unusual. Repeated tripping is a message.

Common causes include:

- **Overloaded circuits.** Several high power appliances on one circuit, such as a kettle, microwave and air fryer running together in the kitchen.
- **A faulty appliance.** If the trip happens every time a particular appliance is switched on, that appliance is the likely cause.
- **Moisture.** Water getting into an outdoor socket, a bathroom fitting or a light near an AC unit that is leaking condensate.
- **Damaged wiring or a loose connection** inside a wall, socket or fitting.

What you can do: unplug everything on the affected circuit, reset the breaker, then plug appliances back in one at a time. If it trips with nothing plugged in, or you cannot find a pattern, stop resetting it and call an electrician. Repeatedly forcing a breaker back on is not a fix.

If water is involved, especially from a leaking AC unit, see our article on [why your AC is not cooling](/zaman/blog/ac-not-cooling-common-causes/), which also covers drain line problems.

## Warm sockets, switches and plugs

A socket or plug should never feel more than slightly warm. Warning signs include:

- A faceplate that is hot to the touch
- Brown or black marks around the socket holes
- A plug that feels loose or falls out easily
- Crackling or buzzing when something is plugged in

These usually point to a loose connection or a worn socket. Loose connections create heat, and heat damages insulation over time. Stop using that socket, switch off its circuit if you know which one it is, and have it inspected.

## A burning smell or smoke

A smell of hot plastic or burning with no obvious source is the most urgent sign on this list. If you notice it:

1. Switch off and unplug anything nearby that could be the source.
2. If the smell is strong, or you see smoke, switch off the main isolator if you can do so safely, and leave the area.
3. In an emergency call the Civil Defence emergency number and alert building security.
4. Do not switch power back on until an electrician has found the cause.

## Flickering or dimming lights

Lights that flicker can be harmless: a loose bulb, an incompatible dimmer or a failing LED driver. Flickering is more worrying when:

- Several lights flicker at once
- Lights dim when the AC or another large appliance starts
- It happens in more than one room

These can indicate a loose connection on a circuit or a problem with the supply. Note when it happens and which rooms are affected, as this helps an electrician diagnose it quickly.

## Building outage or a fault inside your home

When power goes out, first work out whether the problem is yours or the building's.

- Check with neighbours or building security. If the whole building or area is out, it is likely a supply issue. Contact DEWA or your building management.
- If only your home is affected, check the DB board from the outside. A tripped breaker or earth leakage switch points to a fault inside your home.
- If nothing has tripped and only your home is out, contact building management, as the issue may be at the meter or supply cable.

## Labelling your DB board

Many homes in Dubai have DB boards with faded, missing or wrong labels. That makes it hard to isolate a circuit quickly. Ask an electrician to test and clearly label each breaker during their next visit. It is a small job that saves time in an emergency and helps every future repair.

## Extension leads and adaptors

Extension leads are a common cause of overheating. Avoid:

- Plugging high power appliances such as heaters, irons or kettles into extension leads
- Chaining one extension lead into another
- Running leads under rugs or furniture where heat cannot escape
- Cheap multi adaptors without proper safety markings

If you rely on extension leads in a room, that room probably needs more sockets. Adding sockets is work for a qualified electrician.

## When to call an electrician

Call a qualified electrician for repeated tripping, warm or marked sockets, any burning smell, or flickering that affects several lights. If you are renting, tell your landlord or property manager in writing so the issue is on record.
