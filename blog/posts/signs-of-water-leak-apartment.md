Most leaks in apartments do not show up as a burst pipe. They start small, behind tiles, inside walls or above a false ceiling, and only show themselves weeks later. Catching them early limits the damage to your home and to the flats around you.

## Visible signs of a hidden water leak

Walk through your apartment and look for these:

- **Stains on ceilings.** Yellow or brown rings on a gypsum ceiling often mean water is coming from above, from a pipe, an AC drain, or the flat upstairs.
- **Bubbling or peeling paint.** Paint that blisters or flakes, especially low on a wall or near a bathroom, can mean moisture is coming through from behind.
- **Swollen skirting or door frames.** Wood and MDF absorb water and swell. A skirting board that bulges or a door frame that cracks at the bottom is a common clue.
- **Darkening grout.** Grout lines that stay dark long after the shower has dried, or that turn dark in one corner of the floor, can mean water is getting under the tiles.
- **Loose or hollow tiles.** Tiles that sound hollow when tapped, or start to lift, can be a sign that water has broken the bond underneath.
- **Mould.** Black spots in corners, behind furniture, or on the ceiling of a bathroom that is otherwise well ventilated.

## Other clues: smells, sounds and bills

Not every leak leaves a mark you can see.

- **A musty smell** in one room, or inside a kitchen cabinet, that does not go away after airing the room.
- **The sound of running water** when no taps are open, or a toilet that refills by itself.
- **A rising water bill** without any change in how you use water.
- **A water meter that moves** when everything is switched off.

### A simple meter test

Turn off every tap and water-using appliance. Note the reading on your water meter, or take a photo of it. Wait at least an hour without using any water, then check again. If the reading has changed, water is going somewhere. In many buildings the meter is in a service cupboard in the corridor, and building management can show you which one is yours.

## Common sources of leaks in Dubai apartments

- **Shower areas** where waterproofing has failed or grout and sealant have broken down
- **Concealed mixer valves** inside shower walls
- **Toilet cisterns and flush valves** leaking into the bowl or at the connection
- **Flexible hoses** under sinks and behind washing machines
- **AC drain lines** that are blocked or disconnected above the ceiling, a frequent cause of ceiling stains. See [why an AC stops cooling](/zaman/blog/ac-not-cooling-common-causes/) for how drain problems show up.
- **Water heaters** with failing valves or corroded connections

## Who is responsible for fixing a leak

Responsibility depends on where the leak comes from and on your tenancy contract or the building's rules. As a general guide:

- **Inside your apartment, in your pipes or fittings.** Owners are usually responsible. For tenants, the tenancy contract normally says who handles maintenance; often the landlord covers major repairs and the tenant covers minor ones.
- **From the apartment above.** The source is usually that unit's responsibility. Building management can help arrange access.
- **From shared pipes, risers or the roof.** These are normally the building's or owners association's responsibility.

Check your tenancy contract and talk to your landlord or building management early. Take dated photos of the damage, because they help when deciding who pays.

## What to do when you find a leak

1. **Stop the water if you can.** Close the isolation valve under the sink or toilet, or the main valve for the apartment.
2. **Protect your belongings.** Move furniture and electronics away and put down towels or containers.
3. **Switch off electrics near the water.** If water is near sockets, lights or the distribution board, switch off the affected circuit.
4. **Tell the right people.** Inform your landlord or building management, and the neighbour above or below if they may be affected.
5. **Find the source before repairing the damage.** Repainting a stained ceiling without fixing the leak only hides the problem.

Once the source is fixed, let walls and ceilings dry fully before repainting. Damp gypsum may need to be cut out and replaced.

## When to call a plumber

Call a plumber if you see active dripping, if your meter test shows water use with everything off, or if stains keep growing. Finding a hidden leak sometimes means opening a ceiling or removing a few tiles, so it helps to use a contractor who can also repair the gypsum, tiles and paint afterwards.
