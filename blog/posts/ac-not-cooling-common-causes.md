An air conditioner that runs all day without cooling the room is one of the most common maintenance calls in Dubai. The fan blows, but the air is barely cool. Usually the cause is one of a short list of problems, and some you can check yourself.

## Check these first: safe things you can do yourself

1. **Confirm the mode and set point.** Make sure the remote or thermostat is on Cool, not Fan, Dry or Auto, and that the set temperature is below the room temperature.
2. **Look at the filters.** On split units, open the front panel and slide out the mesh filters. If they are grey with dust, wash them in plain water, let them dry fully, and refit them. In ducted systems, the return air grille usually holds a filter that can be checked the same way.
3. **Check the breaker.** In some systems the indoor fan runs on one circuit and the outdoor unit on another. If the outdoor unit has tripped, the fan will blow room-temperature air.
4. **Look at the outdoor unit.** Check that its fan is spinning when the AC is on, and that nothing is blocking the airflow, such as boxes, plants or a cover left on after cleaning.
5. **Close doors and curtains.** A room with a door open to a hot corridor, or full sun through west-facing glass in the afternoon, can overwhelm a unit that is otherwise working fine.

If the AC still is not cooling after these checks, the cause is likely inside the system and needs a technician.

## Dirty coils and blocked airflow

Filters catch most dust, but some always gets through. Over time it builds up on the indoor evaporator coil and the outdoor condenser coil. Dubai's fine sand makes this worse, especially on outdoor units on balconies and rooftops.

A dirty indoor coil cannot absorb heat from the room. A dirty outdoor coil cannot release that heat outside. Either way, cooling drops. Coil cleaning involves removing panels and using suitable coil cleaners, and is part of a proper service. ## Low refrigerant and leaks

An AC does not use up refrigerant. If the level is low, there is a leak somewhere in the pipework, joints or coils. Signs include:

- Ice or frost forming on the indoor coil or on the copper pipes
- A hissing or bubbling sound near the indoor or outdoor unit
- Cooling that gets gradually weaker over weeks
- Oily marks at pipe joints

Refrigerant handling needs a qualified technician with the right equipment. Topping up without finding and fixing the leak only delays the problem. If you see ice on the unit, switch it off and let it thaw, because running a frozen coil can damage the compressor.

## Blocked drain lines and water problems

Split units remove moisture from the air and send it out through a drain pipe. In humid months that is a lot of water. If the drain line is blocked by dust, slime or insects, water backs up into the tray. Some units then stop cooling as a safety measure. Others keep running and start dripping from the indoor unit onto walls and floors.

Water stains below an indoor unit, or a musty smell when the AC starts, often point to a drain issue. Left alone, this can damage gypsum ceilings and paintwork. If you notice stains you cannot explain, read our guide to [signs of a hidden water leak](/zaman/blog/signs-of-water-leak-apartment/).

## Thermostat, sensors and control faults

Sometimes the AC is fine and the controls are not. A faulty thermostat or room sensor can read the wrong temperature and shut off the compressor too early. In ducted systems, a wall thermostat placed near a window or in direct sun can give false readings. A technician can test sensors and controls and check whether the compressor is actually starting when it should.

## Unit size, heat load and duct leaks in villas

An AC can be working perfectly and still struggle because the space asks too much of it. Common causes:

- **Changed layouts.** A wall removed to open up a living area, or a new glass extension, means the existing unit now has to cool a bigger space than it was sized for.
- **Extra heat sources.** Kitchens, server cupboards and rooms with large windows facing west carry more heat.
- **Duct leaks.** In villas with ducted AC, joints in the ductwork above the false ceiling can come loose or tear. Cold air then leaks into the ceiling void instead of reaching the room. Rooms at the end of long duct runs are often the first to feel warm.

Duct problems are hard to spot without opening ceiling access panels, so use a contractor who can also close up the gypsum ceiling afterwards.

## When to call an AC technician

Call a technician if the AC is still not cooling after you have cleaned the filters and checked the settings and breakers, or straight away if you see ice, hear unusual noises from the outdoor unit, or smell burning. Avoid opening sealed panels or touching refrigerant lines yourself.
