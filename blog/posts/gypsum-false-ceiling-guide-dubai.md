A gypsum false ceiling is a second ceiling hung below the concrete slab. It is built from a light steel frame fixed to the slab, with gypsum boards screwed to the underside. The joints are taped, filled and sanded, then the whole surface is painted so it looks like one smooth ceiling.

In Dubai apartments and villas, false ceilings do a lot of practical work. They hide AC ducts, drain pipes and cables. They give you a clean surface for recessed lights. They can also cover an uneven slab or old water stains that paint alone won't fix.

## Types of gypsum false ceiling

### Plain flat ceiling

A flat ceiling dropped a short distance below the slab. This is the most common choice for bedrooms and corridors. It gives a clean look and space above for wiring and downlights.

### Bulkheads

A bulkhead is a boxed section that drops lower than the rest of the ceiling. Bulkheads are often used to hide ducted AC units, run duct lines along a corridor, or frame a kitchen or dining area. They can be straight, stepped or curved.

### Cove lighting ceilings

A cove ceiling has a recessed edge, usually around the room perimeter, where an LED strip sits out of sight. The light washes onto the ceiling instead of shining down. It is popular in living rooms and master bedrooms. The cove needs enough depth to hide the strip and a way to reach the driver if it fails.

### Moisture-resistant board for bathrooms and kitchens

Standard gypsum board absorbs moisture and can sag or stain in a humid room. For bathrooms, kitchens and laundry areas, ask for moisture-resistant board. It is usually green or blue faced. Pair it with an extract fan that actually vents outside, and use a paint suited to wet areas. ## How installation works

A typical gypsum ceiling job follows these steps:

1. **Survey and layout.** The installer measures the room, checks the slab, and marks the ceiling height on the walls with a laser level.
2. **Services first.** Electrical cables for lights, AC ducts and any plumbing above the ceiling should be finished before boards go up. Changing them later means cutting the ceiling.
3. **Framing.** Hangers are fixed to the slab and the main and cross channels are clipped in. A level frame here is what makes a flat ceiling.
4. **Boarding.** Boards are screwed to the frame with joints staggered. Openings are cut for lights, AC grilles and access panels.
5. **Jointing and finishing.** Joints are taped and filled in layers, sanded smooth, then primed and painted.

The finish is where good and average work separate. Poor jointing shows up as lines across the ceiling once the lights are on, especially with cove lighting that grazes across the surface.

## What to check before you start

- **Ceiling height.** Every drop reduces room height. Agree the finished height in writing, and check it allows for any ducts, beams or sprinkler heads.
- **Access panels.** Plan panels under AC units, valves and junction boxes. Without them, a simple repair means cutting a hole.
- **Lighting plan.** Decide where every downlight, spotlight and LED strip goes before boarding. Moving a light later leaves patches.
- **AC grilles and returns.** Make sure supply and return grilles are sized and placed with your AC technician. A bulkhead that restricts airflow can make cooling worse. See our [AC maintenance checklist](/zaman/blog/ac-maintenance-checklist-dubai/) for what to keep accessible.
- **Approvals.** In apartments and many villa communities, check with your landlord, building management or master developer before starting. Changes to fire alarms, sprinklers or MEP services can need approval or permits.

## What to check after installation

- Switch on all the lights at night and look along the ceiling for joint lines, screw pops or waves.
- Check corners and the line where the ceiling meets the wall. Gaps here usually mean a missing or poorly fixed edge trim.
- Open every access panel and confirm it gives real access to what it is meant to.
- Look for any sag, especially in large rooms or under heavy light fittings.
- Confirm smoke detectors, sprinkler heads and AC grilles are sitting flush and working.

## Common problems and their causes

**Cracks along joints** often come from movement in the frame, too few fixings, or jointing done too quickly between coats. **Sagging** usually points to moisture getting into standard board or a frame that wasn't spaced correctly. **Brown stains** mean a leak above the ceiling. Find and fix the source before repainting, or the stain will come back. Our post on [signs of a water leak in an apartment](/zaman/blog/signs-of-water-leak-apartment/) explains what to look for.

Small hairline cracks can often be filled and repainted.
