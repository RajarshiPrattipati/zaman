Gypsum partition walls, often called drywall or gypsum board partitions, are the standard way to divide office and retail space in Dubai. They create meeting rooms, private offices, storage rooms and changing areas without heavy blockwork. This guide covers how they are built, where they work best, and what to plan before work starts.

## How a gypsum partition is built

A typical partition has three parts:

- **A metal stud frame** fixed to the floor and the ceiling or soffit above.
- **Gypsum boards** screwed to one or both sides of the frame.
- **Joint treatment**, where joints and screw heads are taped, filled and sanded, ready for paint or wallpaper.

Insulation can be placed inside the frame for sound control. Boards can be single or double layered depending on the performance needed. Because the frame is hollow, electrical cables and data points run inside the wall.

## Why businesses choose gypsum partitions

### Speed and less disruption

Gypsum partitions are faster to build than blockwork and involve far less wet work and dust. That matters when an office or shop needs to stay open, or when a fit-out has a tight handover date.

### Flexibility

Partitions are lighter than masonry walls and easier to remove or reconfigure later. Businesses that expect to grow or change their layout benefit from this, and landlords often prefer it because the space can be returned to an open plan more easily at the end of a lease.

### A clean finish

Once finished, a gypsum partition looks like any other wall. It can be painted, wallpapered or fitted with wall panels, and it matches well with [gypsum false ceilings](/zaman/blog/gypsum-false-ceiling-guide-dubai/).

## Sound insulation for meeting rooms and offices

A single stud wall with one board each side will reduce noise, but it will not stop conversations being heard next door. For meeting rooms, manager offices and clinics, ask for:

- Acoustic insulation inside the stud cavity
- Double boarding on one or both sides
- Partitions that run to the structural slab above, not just to the false ceiling
- Sealed gaps at the floor, ceiling and around sockets and frames

That third point is often missed. If a partition stops at the false ceiling, sound travels over the top through the ceiling void. Running the wall up to the slab, or adding acoustic barriers in the void, makes a big difference.

## Fire-rated board

Some areas of a building, such as escape routes, electrical rooms or walls between separate tenancies, may need fire-rated partitions. These use fire-resistant gypsum board and a specific build up of layers and sealing. Whether you need them depends on your building, your layout and the requirements of the authorities and building owner, so confirm this at the design stage with your fit-out designer, landlord or building management. Do not assume a standard board will meet those requirements.

## Doors, glass and combined partitions

Many offices combine gypsum with glass to keep spaces bright.

- **Solid gypsum walls with doors** for private offices and storage
- **Gypsum with a glass panel or strip** for meeting rooms that need some privacy and some light
- **Full height glass fronts** set into gypsum side walls for a more open feel

Door openings need a properly reinforced frame so doors do not loosen over time. Heavy wall mounted items such as TVs, shelving or cabinets need timber or metal backing installed inside the partition before boarding, so plan their positions early.

## Approvals and building requirements

Commercial fit-outs in Dubai usually need approval before work starts. Requirements vary between buildings, free zones and master developers. Before building partitions:

- Check your lease and get written approval from your landlord.
- Contact building management for their fit-out rules, working hours and any deposits.
- Ask whether drawings need to be submitted, and to whom.
- Remember that changes affecting fire safety, AC or electrical systems can need further approvals or permits.

Allow time for this in your schedule. Starting work before approval can lead to delays or having to undo it.

## Planning around AC, lighting and power

A new wall changes how a space works.

- **AC:** Each new room needs a supply and return air path. A closed room with no return will be stuffy or hard to cool. Your AC contractor may need to add or move diffusers.
- **Lighting:** Check that every new room has its own lights and switch, and that fittings are not cut in half by the new wall.
- **Power and data:** Mark socket, data and switch positions before boarding, since adding them later means cutting the wall.
- **Sprinklers and smoke detectors:** New rooms may need these moved or added. This must be handled by approved specialists.
