In Dubai, the AC is the most important system in the house. It runs for most of the year and works hardest in the months when you can least afford a breakdown. A unit that is dirty or poorly maintained uses more power, cools less, and is more likely to leak water or fail completely in peak summer.

The best time to service your AC is before the hottest months arrive, when technicians are less busy and you can fix problems calmly. This checklist splits the work into what you can do yourself and what needs a technician.

## What owners and tenants can check

These checks need no tools beyond a ladder and a torch. Always switch the unit off before touching it.

### Filters

Dirty filters are the most common cause of weak airflow. On most split units, the filters slide out from behind the front panel.

- Remove the filters and check them against the light.
- Wash with lukewarm water and mild soap, rinse, and let them dry fully before refitting.
- In dusty periods, check them more often than you normally would.

On ducted systems, the filter is usually behind a return air grille in the ceiling or wall. Some open easily; others need a technician.

### Vents and grilles

- Make sure furniture, curtains and boxes are not blocking supply or return grilles.
- Look for black spots around grilles. These can be dust or mould, and both are worth cleaning.

### Drain line signs

Every AC produces water as it cools. That water runs through a drain line. When the line blocks, water backs up and leaks.

- Look for drips from the indoor unit, damp patches on the wall below it, or water stains on the ceiling under ducted units.
- Listen for gurgling sounds.

If you see any of these, call a technician. Water from a blocked AC drain often looks like a plumbing leak; see our guide on [signs of a water leak in an apartment](/zaman/blog/signs-of-water-leak-apartment/).

### Thermostat and settings

- Check the thermostat or remote is working and the batteries are fresh.
- Confirm the unit is set to cool, not fan or dry, when you want cooling.
- Set a sensible temperature. Very low settings make the unit run nonstop without cooling the room much faster.

### Outdoor unit

For split units with an accessible outdoor unit:

- Clear away leaves, plastic bags and debris around it.
- Make sure nothing is stored against it that would block airflow.
- Look for heavy dust build-up on the fins, but don't spray water at high pressure, as fins bend easily.

## What a technician should do

A proper AC service goes beyond what you can safely do at home. Ask your technician to cover:

1. **Deep cleaning of the indoor coil.** Dust and grime on the coil reduce cooling and can cause smells.
2. **Cleaning the blower wheel.** A dirty fan moves less air.
3. **Flushing and clearing the drain line and tray.** This prevents leaks and mould.
4. **Cleaning the outdoor condenser coil.** A clogged condenser makes the compressor work harder.
5. **Checking refrigerant performance.** A technician can measure temperatures and pressures to see if the system is low on gas, which usually means there is a leak to find.
6. **Electrical checks.** Inspect terminals, capacitors and wiring for heat damage or loose connections.
7. **Duct and insulation checks** on ducted systems. Damaged insulation can cause sweating and ceiling stains.
8. **Testing the thermostat and controls.**

Ask for a short note of what was found and done. It helps you track recurring problems and is useful if you are a tenant reporting issues to your landlord.

## Warning signs to act on early

- The room takes much longer to cool than it used to.
- Air from the vents feels only slightly cool.
- The unit trips the breaker or makes new noises.
- Ice forms on pipes or the indoor coil.
- Water drips from the unit or ceiling.
- A musty or burning smell when the AC starts.

If your AC is running but not cooling, our post on [why an AC stops cooling](/zaman/blog/ac-not-cooling-common-causes/) explains the usual causes.

## When to call a contractor

Filter cleaning and clearing around the unit are fine for owners to do. Coil cleaning, drain flushing, gas checks and electrical work need a trained technician with the right tools.
