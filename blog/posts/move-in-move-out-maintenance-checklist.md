Moving into or out of a rental in Dubai is busy, and maintenance is easy to rush. A careful check at both ends of a tenancy protects tenants and landlords. It makes the condition of the property clear, reduces disagreements over the security deposit, and catches small problems before they become expensive ones.

This checklist works for apartments and villas. Use it as a walk through, room by room.

## Before you start: document everything

Good records matter more than anything else on this list.

- Take clear, dated photos and short videos of every room, including floors, walls, ceilings, inside cupboards and inside appliances.
- Photograph existing damage up close and from a distance so its location is clear.
- Note meter readings where relevant.
- Keep a written list of issues and share it with the other party by email or through the property manager, so there is a record of when it was sent.

How deposit deductions are handled depends on your tenancy contract and the agreement between both sides. If there is a dispute, the clearer your records are, the easier it is to resolve.

## Walls, paint and ceilings

Walls take the most wear in a rental. Check for:

- Scuffs, marks and nail or screw holes
- Patches where paint has peeled or bubbled
- Cracks at corners or above doors and windows
- Damp stains or mould, especially in bathrooms and behind wardrobes
- Ceiling stains, sagging gypsum boards or cracks along board joints

Many contracts expect the property to be returned in a condition similar to when it was received, allowing for normal wear. Small holes and marks are often filled and touched up, while larger areas may need a full wall repaint so the colour matches. Our guide to [choosing interior paint for Dubai's climate](/zaman/blog/interior-paint-dubai-climate/) explains which finishes hold up best.

Ceiling stains deserve attention at move in, since they can point to an AC drain or plumbing leak above.

## AC units

AC is the system tenants rely on most, so test it properly.

- Run every unit and check that it cools within a reasonable time
- Listen for rattling, grinding or clicking
- Look for water marks or drips below indoor units
- Check that filters are clean and that thermostats respond
- Ask when the units were last serviced

Many tenancy contracts and building rules specify who is responsible for routine AC servicing, so check yours. A service at move in gives you a clean starting point. See our [AC maintenance checklist](/zaman/blog/ac-maintenance-checklist-dubai/) for what a proper service should include.

## Plumbing and bathrooms

- Run every tap and shower and check water pressure and hot water
- Flush toilets and check that cisterns refill and stop
- Look under sinks for drips, stains or swollen cabinet bases
- Check that drains clear quickly in sinks, showers and floor drains
- Inspect silicone around baths, trays and basins for mould or gaps
- Look for cracked grout or loose tiles in wet areas

A slow drain or a small drip at handover is cheap to fix now and expensive if it causes water damage to the flat below later.

## Electrical

You do not need to open anything to do a useful check. Never open the DB board yourself.

- Test every light switch and socket (a phone charger works)
- Check that the DB board breakers are labelled
- Note any sockets that are loose, cracked or discoloured
- Test extractor fans in bathrooms and the kitchen
- Check doorbells, intercoms and any built in appliances

Report anything warm, sparking or marked straight away rather than waiting for move out.

## Floors, tiles, doors and fittings

- Look for chipped or cracked tiles and tap a few to check for hollow sounds
- Check wooden or laminate floors for swelling near bathrooms and kitchens
- Open and close every door, window and cupboard and check locks
- Test sliding balcony doors and their seals
- Check curtain rails, wardrobe rails and kitchen hinges

## Deep cleaning

Deep cleaning is usually expected at move out and is worth doing at move in too. It should cover kitchen cabinets, inside appliances, bathroom tiles and grout, AC grilles, windows and balconies. A professionally cleaned property is easier to inspect, as stains and damage are clearer once dirt is gone.

## Getting the work done

The simplest approach at move out is to have touch-ups, minor repairs and cleaning done together, a few days before the final inspection.
