Planning a Bathroom Renovation in Dubai: A Practical Guide

A bathroom is a small room with a lot going on. Water supply, drainage, electrics, ventilation, waterproofing and finishes all meet in a few square metres. That is why bathroom renovations go wrong when people start with the tiles and work backwards. Good planning starts with what you cannot see once the job is finished.

Decide the scope of your bathroom renovation

Before getting quotes, decide which of these you are doing:

  • Cosmetic refresh. New fittings, mirror, lighting, paint and possibly regrouting. The layout and pipework stay as they are.
  • Retile and replace. Remove old tiles and fixtures, renew waterproofing, retile, and fit new sanitaryware in the same positions.
  • Full remodel. Move the shower, toilet or basin, convert a bathtub to a walk-in shower, or change the room layout.

Each step up adds work behind the walls and under the floor. Moving a toilet, for example, depends on where the soil stack runs and whether the floor allows the right fall for the drain. In apartments this is often limited. If your tiles are only stained or the grout is failing, see whether to regrout or retile before committing to a full strip-out.

Approvals from landlord, building management and developer

In Dubai, most residential buildings and communities have rules about renovation work. Before any work starts:

  1. Tenants need written approval from the landlord. Many landlords will not allow layout changes at all.
  2. Owners in apartment buildings usually need approval from building management or the owners association. They may ask for drawings, a contractor's trade license, and details of the work.
  3. Villa owners in managed communities often need approval from the master developer or community management, especially for plumbing or structural changes.

Plumbing and drainage changes, and anything affecting shared services, can need further permits. Check the requirements with your building or community first. Buildings also set working hours and rules for noise, debris removal and lift use.

Plumbing and drainage come first

Once approvals are in place, the first real work is behind the walls:

  • Inspect existing supply pipes and replace old or corroded sections while the walls are open.
  • Fit new concealed valves and mixers for showers, at the correct height and depth for the final tile thickness.
  • Check that floor drains and the shower area have enough slope so water does not pool.
  • Pressure-test the new pipework before anything is covered up.

Fixing a leaking pipe after tiling means breaking tiles. Testing at this stage is cheap insurance.

Waterproofing the bathroom properly

Waterproofing is the single most important step in a bathroom renovation. A leak into the flat below, or into the wall of the next room, can cause damage far beyond the bathroom itself.

A proper job includes:

  • A waterproof membrane across the whole floor, turned up the walls at the edges
  • Full-height or near full-height waterproofing in the shower area
  • Reinforcing tape or bands at corners, joints and around pipe penetrations
  • A flood test, where the floor is filled with water and left to confirm there are no leaks, before tiling

Ask any contractor how they waterproof and whether they flood test. A vague answer is a warning sign.

Ventilation, electrics and lighting

Dubai bathrooms often have no window, so the exhaust fan does all the work of removing moisture. An undersized or blocked fan leads to mould on the ceiling and peeling paint. If the fan is noisy or weak, replace it during the renovation and check where it vents to.

Electrical work in wet areas must be done safely. Sockets, shaver points and light fittings need to be suitable for bathrooms and placed away from water. Water heaters should be on their own properly protected circuit. This is work for a qualified electrician.

If you have a gypsum ceiling, use moisture-resistant board, and leave an access panel where valves or the fan need servicing.

Choosing bathroom tiles and fittings

Practical points that matter more than looks:

  • Floor tiles should have a slip-resistant finish, especially in the shower area.
  • Porcelain tiles absorb less water than standard ceramic and suit wet areas well.
  • Large tiles mean fewer grout lines to clean, but the floor must be very flat, and they are harder to slope towards a drain.
  • Grout in showers should be suitable for wet areas. Epoxy grout resists staining but costs more and needs skilled fitting.
  • Wall-hung toilets and basins make floors easier to clean but need a concealed frame and enough wall depth.

The right sequence of work

A typical order for a full bathroom renovation:

  1. Approvals and protection of surrounding areas
  2. Strip out old tiles, fixtures and ceiling
  3. Plumbing and drainage changes, then pressure test
  4. Electrical first fix
  5. Waterproofing, then flood test
  6. Floor screed and tiling
  7. Gypsum ceiling and painting
  8. Fit sanitaryware, accessories and electrical fittings
  9. Final checks for leaks, drainage and sealant
